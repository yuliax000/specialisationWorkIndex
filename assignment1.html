HomePage Assignment 2

Scenario: Musical Toy for Toddlers

The primary audience is toddlers (approximately 2–5 years old). As they have limited language skills, the website should provide an immersive, visually guided experience that can be understood with minimal text. Bright colours, playful and recognisable icons, and engaging visual effects will be used to capture children's attention and maintain their interest.

Exploration is central to the experience. Since toddlers have little prior experience using websites, they learn through intuitive discovery rather than relying on existing knowledge. Therefore, interactions should be simple, intuitive and easy to discover, allowing children to understand the interface naturally through play.

Response

The website is divided into five horizontal sections. Four sections represent different weather elements, each with its own icon and instrument timbre, while the bottom section functions as percussion.

When users click a section or press its corresponding keyboard key, small weather icons fall onto the grass. Once they land, they trigger a drum sound, a randomly selected pitch and the corresponding instrument timbre. Notes are sustained, allowing sounds to overlap and gradually form layered harmonies.

Visual feedback is immediate, with icons appearing and falling as soon as users interact. However, sound is only triggered when the icons reach the ground, reinforcing the connection between the animation and the musical outcome.

Visual feedback is immediate, with icons appearing and falling as soon as users interact. Sound is intentionally triggered only when the icons reach the ground, strengthening the relationship between movement and sound while making the interaction feel more satisfying.

The purpose of the website is to encourage toddlers to explore music through simple, playful interactions in an intuitive, low-pressure environment. By combining visual interaction with sound, the project introduces music-making as a creative activity that anyone can enjoy without prior musical knowledge.

The worth of the project lies in its accessibility, learnability and playfulness. As young children have limited experience using digital interfaces, interactions are designed to be easy to understand through visual guidance, familiar icons and immediate feedback rather than written instructions.

Framing

The project uses natural metaphors instead of a traditional button-based interface, transforming weather elements into musical instruments rather than piano keys. It aims to connect music with visual and sensory experiences while encouraging exploration instead of traditional music creation. There are no right or wrong outcomes, allowing children to create harmonious music through experimentation.

The characterisation is playful and friendly. As the target audience is toddlers, the interface contains very little text and instead relies on bright colours, recognisable icons, animation and sound to guide interaction.

Randomness is used as a core technique to generate musical variation. Users interact with the instrument through both mouse and keyboard input, while polyphonic sound allows notes to overlap and form harmonies. Rather than providing complex musical controls or parameter adjustments, randomly generated pitches are constrained to a pentatonic scale, ensuring the music remains harmonious while encouraging playful exploration.

Interaction Design Analysis

Example 1

WeAreZizo

We Are Zizo is a website that aims to tell a branding story. What makes this website unique is its use of the scroll wheel as the main interaction, guiding users through the entire story. Compared with traditional click-and-drag interactions, scrolling feels much smoother and more natural. The website also creates a 3D navigation effect, making the experience more immersive and taking users on a journey of discovery.

To encourage users to scroll, the website uses a simple visual cue—a downward arrow—instead of text instructions. This makes the interaction more intuitive and language-independent. I was also impressed by the micro-interactions throughout the website. Hovering over different elements triggers subtle changes in perspective, giving users a stronger sense of control and making the experience feel more engaging. Although users feel that they are freely exploring the website, their journey is actually carefully guided by the narrative structure designed by the creators.

Example 2

Ology

This website uses bright colors and playful patterns to attract children’s attention. I particularly like its cartoon-style icons based on recognizable objects and its layout, which resembles a children’s magazine. Most visual elements have rounded edges, creating a friendly and safe feeling. The playful typography also makes the website more appealing to young users. In terms of information design, the content is organized into clearly separated blocks, using different sizes and arrangements to create hierarchy. I think this suits children’s reading habits well, as they may prefer scanning and jumping between blocks to find interesting information rather than reading everything line by line. This website could be my visual reference, especially the color palette.

Example 3

Gear

I really like how this website uses simulated physics to create rhythm. Mapping the bouncing balls to different timbres is a creative idea that helps the experience avoid becoming repetitive. The letter labels for the sounds were sometimes confusing to me, but they may give more experienced users greater freedom to experiment. I also like the website’s graphics and color palette. Its simple layout and bright colors make it feel like a toy and encourage interaction. The animation is especially effective because it visualizes how the rhythm is created, making the connection between movement and sound easier to understand. I will take inspiration from the visualization of this website.

Example 4

Cycle

What impressed me most about this tool is its use of generative art and its immersive experience. I am always drawn to websites that focus on intuitive interaction rather than relying on extensive written instructions. Since my project, a music toy for toddlers, is designed for users who cannot understand large amounts of text, I naturally look for inspiration from tools that combine visuals with sound. The automatically generated lines further simplify the interaction while providing users with continuous positive feedback. I also noticed that the website carefully limits its choice of timbre and pitch so that everything sounds harmonious. Although this sacrifices some precision, it allows users with no musical knowledge to create satisfying results.

The website is particularly successful at encouraging exploration. When I first opened it, there were no instructions or tutorials. Instead, I instinctively began clicking and dragging to experiment, and I was able to understand how the website worked simply through its responses to my actions. This approach makes the experience feel natural, playful, and rewarding. One aspect I would like to learn from is how intuitive the experience feels.
